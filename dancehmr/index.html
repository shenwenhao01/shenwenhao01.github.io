Read the abstract +

Monocular video human mesh recovery is essential for digital humans, avatar animation, and embodied simulation, where both temporal stability and expressive whole-body motion are required. Existing video HMR methods produce coherent body motion but often overlook detailed hand articulation, while image-based whole-body methods recover SMPL-X meshes independently per frame, often leading to jittery and inaccurate hand motion.

We present DanceHMR, a temporally coherent whole-body HMR framework for challenging in-the-wild monocular videos. DanceHMR unifies body context and part-specific hand observations through residual body-hand fusion, enabling stable body motion and detailed hand recovery within a single temporal architecture. We further introduce close-up-aware augmentation to improve robustness under upper-body framing. Experiments on whole-body and body-only benchmarks demonstrate improved hand reconstruction, competitive body accuracy, and temporally stable, 2D-consistent SMPL-X motion in real-world videos.

03Method

Residual body-hand fusion: Local hand cues refine a body-anchored representation, with body and temporal context supporting recovery under occlusion or blur.

Temporal reconstruction: Joint temporal modeling produces coherent SMPL-X body and hand motion.

Close-up-aware augmentation: Simulated upper-body crops and hand truncation improve robustness to close-up videos.

04Training & Evaluation

DanceHMR is trained with a two-stage curriculum on motion capture, synthetic, and in-the-wild video data, including BEDLAM, MOYO, ARCTIC, EgoBody, AMASS, WHAC-A-Mole, 3DPW, and Human3.6M. The second stage places greater emphasis on high-quality SMPL-X and hand-centric data to refine fine-grained hand articulation.
